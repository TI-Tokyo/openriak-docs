</div><ol class="mailing-list-replies"><li class="mailing-list-reply"><details id="msg05285" class="mailing-list-message"><summary><header class="mailing-list-message__header"><span class="mailing-list-message__author">Jeff Pollard</span><time datetime="2011-10-24T15:33:27-07:00">October 24, 2011 at 3:33 PM</time><span class="mailing-list-message__subject">Re: User authentication and related issues</span></header></summary><div class="mailing-list-message__content"><div class="mailing-list-message__body">We&#x27;ve solved this before a few ways (to give some concrete examples):

   1. On EC2 behind an ELB (which is inherently public on the internet) we
   ran HAProxy on each Riak node, proxying some other port to Riak&#x27;s port 8098.
    The ELB&#x27;s public port was 8098, but it translated that to the port for
   HAProxy.  HAProxy was then running HTTP basic auth in our case, but there
   are plenty of options for you from there.
   2. In our own hardware, we ran Riak through our own load balancer and
   just restricted access to only inside our LAN and our static office IP.
