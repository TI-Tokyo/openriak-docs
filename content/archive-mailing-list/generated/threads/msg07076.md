</div><ol class="mailing-list-replies"><li class="mailing-list-reply"><details id="msg07077" class="mailing-list-message"><summary><header class="mailing-list-message__header"><span class="mailing-list-message__author">Kresten Krab Thorup</span><time datetime="2012-03-29T07:51:22-07:00">March 29, 2012 at 7:51 AM</time><span class="mailing-list-message__subject">Re: Link or Secondary Indexes?</span></header></summary><div class="mailing-list-message__content"><div class="mailing-list-message__body">It all depends on how you use it. At scale, the number of disk accesses and
round trips may be quite different from what you&#x27;d expect.

If you &quot;usually&quot; would want to follow the links, then you&#x27;re probably better
off denormalizing the data in Riak so you can get the information you need in
fewer round trips and disk reads. I.e. embed the linked to information directly
in your objects.

Ive seen comments here on the list that links are bad if you expect more than
~1000 per object, ... If you need them followed often then you only want max 10
... Remember that each individual link-following is another disk read.
Secondary indexing scales much better to large numbers, but has the drawback
that a query hits 1/N of your vnodes (typically all machines). For many kinds
of indexing riak_search is fundamentally a superior solution, but it has some
operational drawbacks in recovery from node crashes.

So Either way, a de-normalized &quot;document oriented&quot; or &quot;pre-cooked&quot; data model
will perform better. Then secondary indexing or links can be used for more
infrequent ad-hoc queries.

Kresten

Neither is better (or worse) for &quot;storing&quot; those bits of information. The
question is how you expect to use that data once it is stored. Let&#x27;s take a
look at friend relationships (note: I don&#x27;t use Facebook, so I might be missing
a subtle feature with this example)...

Do you merely plan on following a list of friends to other pages quickly? For
example, on your page, you see a list of your friends quickly, be able to click
them, and go to their page? If so, the links are quite nice. Links are nice for
being able to follow in a query as well. This is a good example of using links
to perform link walking:

<a href="http://basho.com/blog/technical/2010/02/24/link-walking-by-example/">http://basho.com/blog/technical/2010/02/24/link-walking-by-example/</a>

Or, do you plan on doing reverse lookups by friends? For example, do you want
to say &quot;find every who has Tony as a friend&quot; and get a list of users? Note:
this is not asking who is a friend of Tony (you&#x27;d just lookup Tony and get his
list of friends). In this case 2i works nicely, as do other query methods:
search, a general MapReduce, etc. But links would be an extremely poor choice.

Does this help? This page might also be applicable to your question:

<a href="http://wiki.basho.com/MapReduce-Search-2i-Comparison.html">http://wiki.basho.com/MapReduce-Search-2i-Comparison.html</a>

If you plan on just doing MapReduce queries without 2i, and don&#x27;t care about
link walking, then just storing a typical JSON object and not using either is
perfectly fine as well.

On Thu, Mar 29, 2012 at 8:52 AM, Jeffrey Massung &lt;j...@basho.com&gt; wrote:

<blockquote>Buriwoy,

Neither is better (or worse) for &quot;storing&quot; those bits of information.
The question is how you expect to use that data once it is stored. Let&#x27;s
take a look at friend relationships (note: I don&#x27;t use Facebook, so I might
be missing a subtle feature with this example)...

Do you merely plan on following a list of friends to other pages
quickly? For example, on your page, you see a list of your friends quickly,
be able to click them, and go to their page? If so, the links are quite
nice. Links are nice for being able to follow in a query as well. This is a
good example of using links to perform link walking:

<a href="http://basho.com/blog/technical/2010/02/24/link-walking-by-example/">http://basho.com/blog/technical/2010/02/24/link-walking-by-example/</a>

Or, do you plan on doing reverse lookups by friends? For example, do
you want to say &quot;find every who has Tony as a friend&quot; and get a list of
users? Note: this is not asking who is a friend of Tony (you&#x27;d just lookup
Tony and get his list of friends). In this case 2i works nicely, as do
other query methods: search, a general MapReduce, etc. But links would be
an extremely poor choice.

Does this help? This page might also be applicable to your question:

<a href="http://wiki.basho.com/MapReduce-Search-2i-Comparison.html">http://wiki.basho.com/MapReduce-Search-2i-Comparison.html</a>

If you plan on just doing MapReduce queries without 2i, and don&#x27;t care
about link walking, then just storing a typical JSON object and not using
either is perfectly fine as well.
